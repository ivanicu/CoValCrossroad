R768 · can the extension be ORDERED at all, or is it a set the definition cannot rank?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-05.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R767's NEXT said "the k-curve peaks at k = 6 (eff/MDE 1.311), not at the released core's k = 4
(1.264)", and registered the paired k6 - k4 difference as the next quantity.

⛔ CHECK #370 KILLED THE PREMISE FROM MY OWN COMMITTED ARTIFACT, IN ONE ARITHMETIC STEP:
      k = 4  eff 0.013745      k = 6  eff 0.013681      difference  -0.000063
   **ON THE EFFECT ITSELF THE CURVE PEAKS AT k = 4.** `eff/MDE` orders arms by `eff / sd`, because
   `MDE = z*sd/sqrt(n)` and nothing else varies -- implied sds are 0.1208 (k=4) and 0.1159 (k=6).
   So "k = 6 outscores k = 4" is a statement about the DENOMINATOR, and I wrote it while the two
   effects were printed side by side in my own table. **SIXTH NEXT line this arc killed by the first
   check of the round that acted on it.**

WHAT IS ACTUALLY OPEN, AND IT IS BIGGER THAN THE PAIR. `eff/MDE` is the right statistic for
ADMISSION -- is this effect resolvable at all -- and the wrong one for ORDERING. Nothing in this
campaign has asked whether the extension's members can be ORDERED. The deliverable presents
`coval_core` as the released core; the page carries orderings; and **the pairwise question has never
been posed**: every comparison is an arm against the BASELINE.
⚠ AND THE ANSWER IS NOT FORCED. An arm-vs-arm paired difference shares prompt-level variance between
  its two sides, so its MDE can be far SMALLER than either arm's MDE against the baseline. A gap of
  +0.0023 (`coval_core` - `topw_k6`) may well be resolvable even though each arm's own MDE is ~0.010.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  `eff/MDE` RANKS BY `eff/sd`. With n fixed and z fixed, MDE is a constant times sd, so ordering by
    eff/MDE is ordering by eff/sd -- it REWARDS LOW VARIANCE, not high effect. Two arms with equal
    effects order purely by their noise. That is algebra, and it is why R767's NEXT was wrong.
D2  A PAIRED ARM-VS-ARM MDE IS NOT THE DIFFERENCE OF TWO ARM-VS-BASELINE MDEs.
    var(a - b) = var(a) + var(b) - 2cov(a,b), and a and b are scored on the same prompts, so the
    covariance is large and the paired sd is SMALL. Predicting the pairwise verdicts from the
    marginal MDEs is therefore invalid in BOTH directions, and the round must measure them.
D3  THE PAIRWISE MATRIX IS ANTISYMMETRIC. 31 pairs, not 62; and a verdict for (a,b) determines (b,a).
    Reporting both halves would double-count in the multiplicity correction.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: a pairwise difference could be resolvable merely because
the two arms share most of their criteria -- nested sets differ on few prompts, so their difference
vector is mostly zeros and its sd is tiny. That would make resolution a function of OVERLAP, not of
quality. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: report each pair's criterion-set overlap beside its verdict,
and the correlation between overlap and |eff|/MDE across all 31 pairs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · THE EXTENSION IS AN UNORDERED SET. No pair of the five committed members is resolvably ordered
    after multiplicity. The definition admits four arms and CANNOT say which is better, including
    whether the released core is best. The page's orderings need that scope.
B · IT IS ORDERED, AND THE PAIRING BUYS THE RESOLUTION. At least one committed pair resolves, because
    the paired sd is far below the marginal one -- and then the released core's position is a
    measurable fact rather than an assumption.
C · RESOLUTION TRACKS OVERLAP. The pairs that resolve are the ones sharing the most criteria, so the
    matrix measures set similarity rather than quality, and no ordering claim is admissible.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> 0 of 10 committed pairs survive BH
  B -> >= 1 survives, and its overlap is not extreme within the 31-pair distribution
  C -> the surviving pairs are the top-overlap pairs; corr(overlap, |eff|/MDE) is large and positive

CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POSITIVE  `coval_core` vs `gen_sham` -- a gap of roughly 0.07 in A2 -- must return BEATS. BAND,
          COMPUTED: an instrument returning BEATS for everything also returns it for the g=0 and
          PLACEBO cells below; one returning it for nothing fails here. Unreachable from either end.
g=0       an arm compared against ITSELF: eff exactly 0.0, verdict not BEATS.
NEGATIVE  destroy the pairing -- permute prompts on one side, 200 draws -- and report the MDE
          INFLATION ratio. World excluded: "the paired MDE is small for reasons unrelated to the
          pairing", which would make D2's mechanism wrong.
SHAM      ingredient ABSENT, not inverted. Ingredient = THE PER-ARM sd in the ranking. Re-rank the k
          family with every arm's sd replaced by the POOLED sd, and report how many adjacent
          transpositions separate that order from the eff/MDE order. If the order changes, the
          ranking was sd-driven -- which is D1, measured rather than asserted.
PLACEBO   `topw_k4` vs `topw_k4_detA`: identical artifacts, so eff must be exactly 0.0.
NOISE     B = 1200 for the CI; the MDE has no bootstrap in it (D1), so no B sweep is run and the
          reason is stated rather than the sweep performed.
UNIT      instrument unit = a PROMPT-PAIRED DIFFERENCE over 968 prompts; claim unit = an ORDERED PAIR
          of arms. 31 pairs from 11 arms; `topw_k4`/`_detA` are one OBJECT (R730) and the placebo
          uses exactly that fact.
