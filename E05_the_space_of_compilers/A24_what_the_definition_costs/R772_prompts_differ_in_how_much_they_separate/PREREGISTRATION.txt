R772 · prompts differ in how much they SEPARATE arms — a multiplicative scale, not an additive nuisance
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-05.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R771 found the two estimands split: the pairwise excess says no factor (-0.0074) while the spectrum
says structure (leading share 0.3791 vs a simulated band [0.2974, 0.3172]). Its NEXT named a shared
nuisance -- "each arm is scored against the same human annotations, so a prompt whose annotators
disagree more depresses each arm's A2 together" -- and registered a partial correlation on annotator
agreement.

⛔ CHECK #374 REFUTES THAT NUISANCE FROM R771's OWN ALGEBRA, BEFORE ANY MEASUREMENT.
   An effect that depresses every arm EQUALLY is ADDITIVE AND COMMON, and R771 proved -- and its
   positive control demonstrated at four loadings -- that a common additive term CANCELS in every
   difference: `d_ab = (A2_a + u) - (A2_b + u) = A2_a - A2_b`. **So the nuisance I registered is the
   exact object R771 showed is invisible here.** EIGHTH closing line this arc needing repair, and the
   first refuted by the round that wrote it.

⭐ WHAT DOES NOT CANCEL IS A MULTIPLICATIVE SCALE. If a prompt's capacity to SEPARATE arms varies --
   `d_ab(p) = c(p) * delta_ab + noise` -- then `c(p)` multiplies every pair's difference together, does
   NOT cancel, and predicts exactly the three things R771 measured: small positive DISJOINT
   correlations, ~zero excess among arm-SHARING pairs (dominated by the shared-arm term), and extra
   spectral concentration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMAND (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  the correlation of |d| across pairs, reported SEPARATELY for the 15 DISJOINT pairs (clean) and
    the 30 arm-sharing pairs (inflated by the shared arm, D2 below). The disjoint block is the test.
E2  the correlation between the per-prompt scale `c(p)` and per-prompt ANNOTATOR AGREEMENT -- which
    answers R771's registered question in the form that survives the algebra.
E3  whether normalising by `c(p)` removes the spectral excess, estimated LEAVE-ONE-PAIR-OUT so that
    no pair is normalised by a scale computed from itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDENTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1 IDENTIFIED and exact.
E2 IDENTIFIED; annotator agreement is computable as the mean pairwise agreement among a prompt's own
   annotators, and prompts with a single annotator are excluded and counted.
E3 ⚠ IDENTIFIED ONLY UNDER LEAVE-ONE-PAIR-OUT. `c(p)` computed from all ten pairs and then used to
   normalise those same ten is **conditioning on the outcome** -- §4's row -- and would shrink the
   correlations mechanically whether or not any scale exists. A quick probe gave 0.3791 -> 0.3330
   under the circular version; **that number is inadmissible and this round does not report it as
   evidence.** Leave-one-pair-out breaks the circularity: pair i is normalised by the mean |d| of the
   other nine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  AN ADDITIVE COMMON TERM CANCELS. Proven in R771 and demonstrated by its own failed plant at four
    loadings. So annotator agreement can only act here MULTIPLICATIVELY, and the registered partial
    correlation on an additive nuisance was answering a question the algebra had already closed.
D2  |d| CORRELATES AMONG ARM-SHARING PAIRS EVEN WITH NO SCALE. `|d_ab|` and `|d_ac|` share arm a, so
    their magnitudes co-move for the same reason their signed values do. **The arm-sharing block is
    therefore NOT evidence for a scale**, and only the 15 disjoint pairs are admissible.
D3  DIVIDING BY A SCALE ESTIMATED FROM THE SAME DATA SHRINKS CORRELATIONS MECHANICALLY. Any drop in
    the leading share under the circular normalisation is uninformative in BOTH directions.
D4  c(p) HAS A FLOOR AT ZERO AND SOME PROMPTS SIT ON IT. A ratio to a near-zero denominator explodes;
    the round reports c's QUANTILES, never its min/max ratio, and normalises with a floored divisor
    whose floor is stated.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: a multiplicative scale and a heteroscedastic noise floor
are observationally similar -- prompts where the JUDGE is noisier inflate every |d| together without
any real difference in separability. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: compute the per-prompt
annotator-level SE of each difference (R770's within component) and report the correlation between
`c(p)` and that SE. If they track, the "scale" is a noise-amplitude map and not a separability map,
and the round says so.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · A REAL SEPARABILITY SCALE. Disjoint |d| correlations are positive and exceed what an
    independence simulation gives; leave-one-pair-out normalisation brings the leading share inside
    the sham band; and `c(p)` does NOT merely track the noise SE. Then "which prompts separate cores"
    HAS an answer and it is a per-prompt scale, not a partition.
B · A NOISE-AMPLITUDE MAP. `c(p)` tracks the within-prompt SE, so the co-movement is heteroscedastic
    noise and separability is not a property of prompts at all.
C · NEITHER. Disjoint |d| correlations sit inside the simulated band and the normalisation changes
    nothing beyond what the sham normalisation does.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> disjoint |d| corr above the simulated band, LOPO-normalised share inside the sham band,
       corr(c, within-SE) < 0.5
  B -> corr(c, within-SE) >= 0.5
  C -> disjoint |d| corr inside the simulated band

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POSITIVE  plant `d_ab -> s(p) * d_ab` with `s` lognormal at sweep width in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0}, and
          require the DISJOINT |d| correlation to rise monotonely and be detected at 1.0.
          BAND, COMPUTED: at width 0 nothing may be detected; at 1.0 it must be. ⚠ AND THE PLANT IS
          MULTIPLICATIVE, because that is the object under test -- the defect R771 caught in its own
          control was planting an additive factor the instrument cannot see.
g=0       width 0: the same machinery must return a disjoint correlation inside its own simulated band.
NEGATIVE  permute `c(p)` across prompts, 200 draws, and normalise by the permuted scale. World
          excluded: "dividing by ANY vector of this distribution lowers the leading share".
SHAM      ingredient ABSENT, not inverted. Ingredient = THE SCALE'S ALIGNMENT WITH THE DATA. Normalise
          by a random vector drawn from `c`'s own empirical distribution, 200 draws -- same marginal,
          same floor, no alignment -- and report the leading-share distribution. The LOPO result must
          beat it.
PLACEBO   `topw_k4` vs `_detA`: |d| identically 0, so it must contribute no correlation and must be
          excluded by construction rather than dividing by zero.
NOISE     the within-prompt SE from R770 is recomputed here and used as the confound axis, not assumed.
UNIT      instrument unit = a PROMPT; intermediate = an ARM PAIR (10); claim unit = the per-prompt
          SCALE, which is a property of the PROMPT. All three named.
