R819 · THE PER-PROMPT RATIO IS NOT ESTIMABLE AS R818 COMPUTED IT
written before the run · NBOOT 1200 · ZEFF 2.801585

PROVOCATION
R818 reported a per-prompt share `(A2_p − floor_p) / (att_p − floor_p)` averaged over prompts, found
it far below the corpus-level share (`coval_core` **0.0617** against **0.5001**), found four arms
BELOW the constant floor, and returned **WORLD C** because the resulting ordering differed from the
corpus-level one at Spearman **+0.9833**. Its NEXT proposed regressing each arm's margin on the span.

⛔ CHECK #421 KILLED THAT NEXT ON ARITHMETIC AND FOUND THE REAL DEFECT.
 (1) The NEXT's hypothesis is self-defeating: if `margin_p = c · span_p` EXACTLY, then every
     per-prompt ratio equals c and the corpus-level ratio equals c too — proportionality produces
     AGREEMENT, not the observed divergence. The NEXT asked a question whose affirmative answer
     would explain the opposite of what was seen.
 (2) ⭐ The divergence is a SMALL-DENOMINATOR artifact. Measured: span mean 0.2368, median 0.2131,
     with **12.8% of prompts below 0.05** and **24.5% below 0.10**. The per-prompt ratio runs to
     **−29.00**. The smallest-span decile averages **−3.105** while the other nine average
     **+0.414**, and that single decile contributes **−503.1% of the total sum** — it flips the
     aggregate's sign on its own.
 (3) Stable estimators agree with the corpus-level number: trimmed **+0.4136**, median **+0.5965**,
     against corpus-level **0.5001**. R818 named this risk and excluded only EXACT zeros (48
     prompts), which is not enough when an eighth of the population sits under 0.05.

ESTIMAND
E1  the span distribution and the ratio's tail: how much of R818's E3 comes from small denominators.
E2  ⭐ the ESTIMATOR CURVE for the per-prompt share: naive mean · trimmed at 5/10/20% · median ·
    winsorised · SPAN-WEIGHTED. [DERIVATION] the span-weighted mean of ratios IS
    `sum(margin)/sum(span)`, i.e. the corpus-level statistic — so the corpus number is already a
    member of this family and is the minimum-variance member. Stating that first is what stops the
    round claiming to have "found" that they agree.
E3  ⭐ does R818's REORDERING (Spearman +0.9833) survive ANY stable estimator?
E4  the retraction: which of R818's E3 claims stand, and what WORLD C rested on.

IDENTIFICATION
E1/E2/E3 exact given the arms and the floor. ⚠ A trimmed or winsorised mean is a DIFFERENT estimand
from the untrimmed one, not a better estimate of it; the round reports the family and names which
member each claim uses, rather than picking one and calling it the answer.

DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
D1 [DERIVATION] span-weighted mean of ratios = corpus-level ratio, exactly. Not a finding.
D2 [DERIVATION] with `margin_p = c · span_p` the entire family collapses to c, so ANY disagreement
   between members proves the margin is NOT proportional to the span — which kills R818's NEXT
   before any regression is run.
D3 the constant arm has margin identically 0, so every member of the family returns exactly 0 for
   it. PLACEBO.
D4 an arm placed at a known share f of each prompt's span must return f under every member. The
   NAIVE mean must ALSO return f when the plant is exact — so the naive estimator is not broken in
   general, only in the presence of noise near a small denominator. That distinction is testable
   and is the positive control's second dose.

WORLDS
A  UNSTABLE: stable members agree with the corpus-level share and the reordering vanishes ->
   R818's E3 and its WORLD C verdict are retracted as a small-denominator artifact.
B  REAL: the reordering survives trimming and the median -> R818's E3 stands.
C  MIXED: some members reorder and some do not -> report the family and claim no single ordering.
CHECKED FIRST: B, because it defends the round already committed.
