R801 · pooled mean or robust across prompts — is that a real choice, or one statistic twice?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-06.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R800 located the variance -- prompt 67.0%, interaction 18.3%, noise 13.9%, criterion 0.9% -- and
closed by asking whether a core selected to be ROBUST ACROSS PROMPTS differs from one selected to
maximise the POOLED MEAN.

CHECK #403 attacked that before designing, and found the question is only well-posed for HALF the
population.

(a) ⛔ `select_core.py:121` loops PER PROMPT and picks that prompt's criteria from that prompt's own
    rubric. Every rubric-derived arm is therefore selected independently on each prompt: **there is
    no cross-prompt objective to vary**, and "robust across prompts versus pooled mean" is not a
    choice those arms can express. R800's NEXT was not well-posed for them, and saying so is part of
    the finding rather than a preamble to it.
(b) ⭐ THE CHOICE IS REAL FOR BLIND ARMS, where ONE criterion set serves all 968 prompts. The release
    supplies `genericpool16`, so the full space of blind 4-criterion cores is **C(16,4) = 1,820**
    subsets -- the same class R787, R788 and R796 built -- and each has both a pooled mean and a
    cross-prompt spread. The frontier between them has never been computed.
(c) ⛔⛔ AND THE ARITHMETIC TRAP IS THE WHOLE RISK. The pooled mean and any robustness statistic are
    functions of the SAME per-prompt vector. If subsets differ from one another mainly by a LOCATION
    SHIFT, then worst-decile = mean − constant and **the two argmaxes coincide by construction**,
    making "robustness" a restatement. The registered control is to regress the robustness statistic
    on the pooled mean across all 1,820 and report the RESIDUAL -- what robustness adds beyond the
    mean -- rather than the raw ranking agreement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISCLOSURE -- what was already known when this was written
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN and quoted: the per-prompt selection loop; the 1,820-subset class from R787/R788/R796;
`genericpool16`'s committed A2 **0.5422329001** and `generic`'s **0.551354** (R789), where `generic`
IS `POOL[0:4]` (R788). **NOT known: any subset's cross-prompt spread**, the frontier, the residual,
or which subset maximises either objective.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  THE ALL-16 SUBSET IS `genericpool16` EXACTLY, so its pooled mean must equal the committed
    0.5422329001, and the subset `(0,1,2,3)` must equal `generic`'s 0.551354. **DERIVATION**, and
    they are this round's two exact OBJECT anchors -- one for the whole pool, one for a named subset.
D2  A ROBUSTNESS STATISTIC THAT IS AN AFFINE FUNCTION OF THE MEAN CANNOT DISAGREE WITH IT. So the
    finding is not "do the rankings correlate" (they must) but "does the residual carry an argmax of
    its own". **DERIVATION**, and it fixes the estimand as the residual, not the correlation.
D3  A WORST-DECILE IS AN ORDER STATISTIC OF 968 NOISY PER-PROMPT VALUES, so it is biased downward and
    its own sampling error is larger than the mean's. **DERIVATION**, and it is why the round reports
    TWO robustness statistics -- the cross-prompt sd and the worst-decile -- rather than trusting an
    extremum alone (§4's *min/max of N draws quoted as an interval*).
D4  ALL 1,820 SUBSETS SHARE THE SAME 968 PROMPTS, so their per-prompt vectors are heavily correlated
    and the spread ACROSS SUBSETS is far smaller than the spread across prompts. **DERIVATION**, and
    it means a "difference between two subsets" must be judged against a paired bootstrap, never
    against the across-prompt sd.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: a subset's cross-prompt sd is partly the ANNOTATOR noise
on each prompt, which is larger for prompts with fewer annotators -- so a subset can look "robust"
merely by being measured on the same prompts as everything else with noise that does not cancel.
CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: the annotator noise per prompt is measured by split-half and
SUBTRACTED from each subset's cross-prompt variance before ranking, and both the raw and
noise-corrected rankings are reported.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMANDS (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  ⭐ for all 1,820 blind 4-subsets: the pooled mean A2 against the human, the cross-prompt sd, and
    the worst-decile mean -- each on the same 968 prompts and all annotators.
E2  ⭐ THE RESIDUAL (D2): regress each robustness statistic on the pooled mean across the 1,820, and
    ask whether the residual has an argmax different from the mean's, and by how much on the mean's
    own scale.
E3  ⭐ the frontier: how many subsets are Pareto-optimal in (mean, robustness), and whether the
    pooled-mean maximiser is among them.
E4  where `generic` = `POOL[0:4]` sits in each ranking, and the well-posedness statement from check
    #403(a): the rubric-derived arms cannot express this choice at all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · ROBUSTNESS IS A SEPARATE AXIS. The residual of the robustness statistic on the pooled mean has an
    argmax different from the mean's, and the cost of choosing it -- the mean given up -- is
    resolvable against a paired bootstrap. Then clause ② must SAY which objective it means.
B · ROBUSTNESS IS A RESTATEMENT. The robustness statistic is near-affine in the mean (residual sd
    small relative to its own range) and the argmaxes coincide. Then the definition need not choose,
    and R800's NEXT dissolves rather than being answered.
C · THE CHOICE EXISTS BUT IS UNRESOLVABLE. The argmaxes differ but the mean given up is inside the
    paired-bootstrap noise. Then the axis is real and this release cannot price it, which is a
    different statement from either A or B and must not be folded into them.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> argmaxes differ AND the mean forgone excludes 0 on a paired bootstrap
  B -> argmaxes coincide for BOTH robustness statistics
  C -> argmaxes differ AND the mean forgone contains 0
  any other pattern (e.g. the two robustness statistics disagree with each other) -> report; CLAIM
  NO WORLD, and report the disagreement as the finding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if OBJECT passes (D1: the all-16 subset reproduces `genericpool16`'s committed 0.5422329001 and the
   subset (0,1,2,3) reproduces `generic`'s 0.551354, both to 1e-9 -- exit 2 otherwise)
   and PLACEBO returns exactly 0 for a subset against itself
   and POSITIVE fires (a planted per-prompt heteroscedastic perturbation that leaves the MEAN
       unchanged must move the robustness statistics -- if it does not, the instrument cannot tell
       the two objectives apart and no comparison is admissible; band computed at two magnitudes)
   and NEGATIVE fires (shuffling the human classes across prompts collapses every subset toward
       chance and destroys the frontier):
        the two robustness statistics disagree on the argmax  -> report; CLAIM NO WORLD (checked FIRST)
        argmaxes coincide for both                            -> WORLD B
        argmaxes differ and the mean forgone excludes 0       -> WORLD A
        argmaxes differ and the mean forgone contains 0       -> WORLD C
        otherwise                                             -> report; claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OBJECT      D1's two exact anchors, to 1e-9. Exit 2, never 0.
PLACEBO     a subset against itself: mean difference exactly 0 and residual exactly 0.
POSITIVE    BAND COMPUTED AT TWO MAGNITUDES: add zero-mean per-prompt noise of sd s to one subset's
            per-prompt vector for s in {0.02, 0.08}; the pooled mean must be unchanged to ~0 while
            the cross-prompt sd and worst-decile must move monotonically. A statistic that does not
            move is not measuring robustness.
NEGATIVE    shuffle the human classes across prompts; every subset's mean must fall toward chance and
            the mean-robustness relation must collapse. World it excludes: "the frontier is a
            property of the criteria's internal structure rather than of their fit to the humans."
CONFOUND    the annotator-noise correction: per-prompt split-half noise subtracted from each subset's
            cross-prompt variance, with raw and corrected rankings both reported.
NOISE FLOOR MEASURED: paired bootstrap over prompts on the difference between the two argmax subsets.
UNIT        instrument unit = a (subset, prompt) A2; claim unit = an OBJECTIVE; E3's unit = a SUBSET.
            Three strings, required unequal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLICITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1,820 subsets x 3 statistics = 5,460 computed quantities, but only ONE comparison is tested: the
mean forgone by the robustness argmax, on a paired bootstrap. The 1,820 are a SEARCH SPACE and the
argmax over them is a selection, so its value is reported with the selection bias named -- the
winner's mean is an extremum of 1,820 correlated draws and is quoted as such, never as an estimate
of that subset's population value.
