R778 · both arm-free covariates were computed on criteria NO ARM USES
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-05.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R776 built `poolspread` and R777 built `orderdisagree`, both from `sat_genericpool16.npz`, and both
returned clean nulls against a family co-movement of 0.59-0.72. R777's NEXT registered a uniformity
check on the `random_k` arms' draws "from the pool".

CHECK #380 KILLS THE PREMISE OF BOTH ROUNDS, AT THE OBJECT, BEFORE ANY DESIGN:
  - the 16-criterion "pool" is PROMPT-BLIND: `core_genericpool16.json` holds ONE identical set for
    all 968 prompts (distinct sets across prompts = 1).
  - `random_k4_s0` uses **3,869 distinct criteria** across 968 prompts and its overlap with the pool
    is **0**. `topw_k4` likewise: **0/968** of its sets are subsets of the pool.
  - the arms draw from the PROMPT'S OWN RUBRIC: **968/968** of `random_k4_s0`'s sets are subsets of
    `core_full.json`, whose per-prompt size is median **15**, range **4 to 39**.

⇒ **Two rounds of "arm-free covariates" measured a criterion set that shares NOTHING with any arm's
  criteria.** The nulls were guaranteed by construction. This is §4's unit rule exactly: the
  instrument's unit was the generic pool's criteria, the claim's unit was the arms' criteria, and the
  two sets are disjoint. Both nulls are retracted as evidence about prompts.

AND THE RIGHT OBJECT SUGGESTS A MECHANISM THE POOL COULD NEVER HAVE SHOWN. Rubric size varies 4 to 39
while k is fixed per arm, so `k / n_rubric` varies by an order of magnitude. Two `random_k4` draws
from a 4-criterion rubric are IDENTICAL; from a 39-criterion rubric they rarely overlap at all. So
`|d|` depends on rubric size, EVERY random family inherits that dependence, and their scales must
co-move -- with no prompt property involved beyond how many criteria the rubric happens to hold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  THE TWO PRIOR NULLS WERE FORCED. A covariate computed on criteria disjoint from every arm's
    cannot correlate with those arms' differences except through whatever the two sets share, and they
    share nothing. **So R776 and R777 measured nothing about the arms**, and their "the pool does not
    explain it" conclusions are retracted -- not because they are false, but because they were never
    tests.
D2  TWO k-SUBSET DRAWS FROM AN n-CRITERION RUBRIC OVERLAP IN EXPECTATION k^2/n, so their difference
    shrinks as n falls and vanishes at n <= k. **A negative correlation between `n_rubric` and any
    random family's |d| is therefore PARTLY FORCED**, and the measurement is (a) its size, (b) whether
    the NON-random families show it too, and (c) whether it explains the M x R co-movement.
D3  AT n_rubric <= k THE DRAW IS DEGENERATE: every arm takes the whole rubric and |d| = 0 exactly.
    Those prompts must be counted and reported, because they are the same object as R772's 223 ties
    seen from the construction side rather than the score side.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: `n_rubric` may correlate with prompt difficulty, so a
correlation with the scale could be difficulty rather than draw geometry. CONTROL IN THE SAME
ITERATION: report corr(`n_rubric`, the baseline arm's A2) and the partial holding it fixed, so a
difficulty explanation is visible rather than assumed absent.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · DRAW GEOMETRY. `n_rubric` correlates with the random families' scales substantially, more than
    with the non-random ones, and conditioning on it materially reduces M x R. Then the entire
    co-movement thread resolves to "how many criteria the rubric holds", which is not a prompt
    property in any interesting sense and closes the line.
B · A RUBRIC STATISTIC, NOT ITS SIZE. `n_rubric` does little but `rubricdisagree` or `rubricspread`
    does what their pool versions could not. Then R776/R777's hypotheses were right and their object
    was wrong.
C · NEITHER. All three rubric covariates behave like the pool ones did. Then the co-movement is not
    explained by any rubric statistic tried, and that is a much stronger statement than the pool
    rounds could make, because this time the covariates are computed on criteria the arms use.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> |corr(n_rubric, scale)| >= 0.30 for at least 3 of the 4 random families AND M x R drops >= 25%
  B -> a rubric statistic reaches >= 0.30 for a majority while `n_rubric` stays < 0.15
  C -> every covariate < 0.15 for a majority of families
