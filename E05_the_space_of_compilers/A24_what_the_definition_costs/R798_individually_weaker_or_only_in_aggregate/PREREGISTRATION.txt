R798 · are the rubric's criteria individually weaker, or does the loss appear only in AGGREGATE?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-06.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R797 measured that `genericpool16` predicts a prompt's own human rankings better than that prompt's
own rubric: **+0.0335 [+0.0251, +0.0420]**, MDE 0.0118, resolved, surviving a size stratification.
Its NEXT asked whether `full`'s criteria are individually weaker predictors or whether the loss
appears only when they are summed.

CHECK #400 attacked the question before designing it and found two things.

(a) ⭐ THE COMPOSITE IS TWO QUANTITIES. A singleton criterion's class is `sign(y_i[a] - y_i[b])` over
    the six response pairs. When a criterion is satisfied EQUALLY by two responses the sign is 0, and
    a 0 can never agree with a human's ±1. So singleton agreement mixes **DISCRIMINATION** (how often
    the criterion produces a non-zero sign) with **ACCURACY** (whether it is right when it does).
    Measured on 300 prompts before designing: the zero-sign share is **0.0444** for `full` and
    **0.0508** for `genericpool16`. ⚠ Small, and it runs AGAINST the pool — so ties cannot explain
    R797's gap, and the decomposition is a control rather than the expected mechanism.
(b) ⛔ AND THE PER-CRITERION QUESTION AS WRITTEN IS NOT IDENTIFIED. Each of `full`'s criteria appears
    on EXACTLY ONE prompt, while each of `genericpool16`'s 16 appears on all 968. So a `full`
    criterion has n = 1 prompt of evidence and cannot be estimated individually at all. The
    identified estimand is the **distribution over criterion INSTANCES**, and the round says so
    rather than reporting per-criterion numbers that do not exist. G1: identification before power.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISCLOSURE -- what was already known when this was written
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN and quoted: R797's aggregate gap and its strata; the two tie shares above (300 prompts, check

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  SINGLETON AGREEMENT = DISCRIMINATION x ACCURACY + 0 x TIE-SHARE, exactly, because a tied pair
    contributes 0 to the agreement count and 1 to the denominator. So `A2_single = (1 - tie) *
    accuracy_on_nontied`. **DERIVATION**, and it is why the two components must be reported beside
    the composite rather than instead of it.
D2  A `full` CRITERION IS OBSERVED ON ONE PROMPT. Its singleton agreement is a single prompt's
    measurement with ~16 annotators, so its own sampling error is large and no per-criterion ranking
    is admissible. **DERIVATION**, and it converts the question from "which criteria" to "what
    distribution".
D3  THE AGGREGATE IS NOT THE MEAN OF THE SINGLETONS. Summing k criteria then taking signs is not the
    same as averaging k singleton classes -- a sum can flip a sign that no individual criterion
    carried. So a singleton gap and an aggregate gap are DIFFERENT quantities and either could be
    larger. **DERIVATION**, and it is exactly what makes worlds A and B distinguishable.
D4  THE POOL'S 16 CRITERIA ARE THE SAME 16 EVERYWHERE, so their instance-level values are 968
    repeated measurements of 16 objects, not 15,488 independent ones. Any interval over pool
    instances must cluster by CRITERION, not treat instances as independent. **DERIVATION**, and it
    is this round's most likely place to overstate precision.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: the two pools' criteria may differ in how many responses
they are satisfied by at all -- a criterion satisfied by none or all of the four responses is inert
regardless of its content. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: report the satisfaction SPREAD per
criterion instance (the sd across the four responses) for both pools, and re-run the comparison on
instances matched on that spread.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMANDS (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  ⭐ the two DISTRIBUTIONS of singleton agreement with the human, over criterion instances:
    mean, sd, quantiles, and the paired-by-prompt difference of the two pools' prompt-level means.
E2  ⭐ D1's DECOMPOSITION: discrimination (1 − tie share) and accuracy-on-non-tied, per pool, each
    with an interval clustered correctly (by prompt for `full`, by CRITERION for the pool, per D4).
E3  ⭐ INDIVIDUAL VERSUS AGGREGATE: the singleton-level gap against R797's aggregate +0.0335. If the
    singleton gap is near zero while the aggregate is resolved, the loss is in how criteria COMBINE.
E4  the satisfaction-spread confound: the spread distribution per pool and the gap on
    spread-matched instances.

SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · INDIVIDUALLY WEAKER. The pool's singleton instances beat `full`'s, resolved, and the gap is of
    the same order as R797's aggregate +0.0335. Then the rubric's disadvantage is in its criteria,
    one at a time, and the aggregate result is a restatement.
B · ONLY IN AGGREGATE. The singleton gap is unresolved or much smaller than the aggregate. Then
    individual criteria are comparable and the loss appears in how they COMBINE -- a claim about the
    rubric's composition rather than its content.
C · THE OPPOSITE. `full`'s singletons are resolvedly BETTER while the aggregate favours the pool.
    Then summing actively destroys what the rubric's criteria individually carry, which would be the
    strongest version of B and must be named separately so it cannot be absorbed into it.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> singleton gap resolved, positive, and >= half the aggregate 0.0335
  B -> singleton gap unresolved, or resolved but < half the aggregate
  C -> singleton gap resolved and NEGATIVE
  any other pattern -> report; CLAIM NO WORLD.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if OBJECT passes (summing ALL of a pool's criteria reproduces that pool's committed aggregate A2 --
   `full` 0.5087225654 and `genericpool16` 0.5422329001, both to 1e-9 -- exit 2 otherwise, since a
   singleton decomposition whose sum disagrees with the whole is decomposing something else)
   and PLACEBO returns exactly the identity: D1's `(1 - tie) * accuracy` equals the composite to 1e-12
   and POSITIVE fires with a computed band (a planted sign-flip on a share of instances degrades
       singleton agreement monotonically and does not move it at share 0)
   and NEGATIVE fires (shuffling the human classes across prompts collapses BOTH pools' singleton
       agreement toward chance):
        singleton gap resolved and negative                  -> WORLD C   (checked FIRST)
        singleton gap resolved and >= 0.01675 (half 0.0335)  -> WORLD A
        singleton gap unresolved or < 0.01675                -> WORLD B
        otherwise                                            -> report; claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OBJECT      both pools' summed-criteria A2 against R793's and R789's committed values, to 1e-9.
            Exit 2, never 0.
PLACEBO     D1's identity, checked numerically: composite == (1 - tie) * accuracy to 1e-12.
POSITIVE    BAND COMPUTED AT BOTH ENDS: flip the sign of a share s of instances' classes, sweep
            s in {0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5}; singleton agreement must be unmoved at 0 and degrade at 0.5.
NEGATIVE    shuffle the HUMAN classes across prompts; both pools' singleton agreement must fall
            toward chance. World it excludes: "singleton agreement measures a property of the
            criteria alone rather than of their fit to THIS prompt's humans."
CLUSTERING  D4: the pool's instances cluster by CRITERION (16 clusters), `full`'s by PROMPT. Both
            intervals are computed on the correct cluster and the naive independent-instance
            interval is printed BESIDE them to show what it would have overstated.
CONFOUND    E4's satisfaction-spread matching.
NOISE FLOOR MEASURED: annotator split-half on each pool's singleton mean.
UNIT        instrument unit = a (prompt, criterion, annotator) judgement; claim unit = a POOL;
            E1's reporting unit = a criterion INSTANCE. Three strings, required unequal.
