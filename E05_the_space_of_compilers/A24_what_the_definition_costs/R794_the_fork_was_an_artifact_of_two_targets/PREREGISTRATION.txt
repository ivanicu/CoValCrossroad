--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R793 closed with a WALL: *"the step is to settle the normalisation by argument ... That is a
formulation act, and it is the first thing in this arc that no further computation can decide."*
§4 says an unchecked wall is UNVERIFIED and never SETTLED, and four walls have fallen in this arc's
last six rounds. CHECK #396 attacked it first, and it took three lines.

⭐ THE WALL IS FALSE. The normalisation problem exists ONLY because `whose_verdicts` compares
`vs HUMAN` (a proportion against a noisy target, ceiling 0.5519) with `vs FULL` (a proportion against
a deterministic one, ceiling 1.0). **Two SAME-TARGET questions answer the clause without any ceiling:**

  Q1  does a core preserve the RUBRIC's verdicts?
      `core vs FULL` = 0.7850, against a random arm's class 0.7342 and a shuffled full 0.4998.
      Both sides scored against the SAME target.
  Q2  does a core track the HUMAN better than the rubric it compresses does?
      `core vs HUMAN` = 0.5665 against `full vs HUMAN` = 0.5087, gap +0.0578.
      Both sides scored against the SAME target.

**Neither needs a ceiling.** So the A/B dichotomy is not a fact about cores; it is a consequence of
one cross-target comparison, and the choice of normalisation R793 called undecidable is a question
that does not have to be asked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  A SAME-TARGET COMPARISON NEEDS NO CEILING. If two quantities are proportions against the SAME
    target, their difference is on the target's own scale and a ceiling divides out of the
    comparison. **DERIVATION**, and it is the whole reason this round exists.
D2  Q1 AND Q2 ARE NOT COMPLEMENTARY. Nothing in the algebra forces `core vs FULL` high to imply
    `core vs HUMAN` low. A core can preserve the rubric AND beat it at predicting the human, because
    the rubric is not a perfect predictor of the human. **DERIVATION**, and it is why the fork can be
    false rather than merely mis-measured.
D3  `core vs FULL` IS INFLATED BY BOTH TRACKING THE HUMAN. Any two arms with signal about the human
    agree with each other more than chance. So Q1's raw number is not evidence that the core tracks
    the RUBRIC specifically. **DERIVATION**, and it is the confound this round must control.
D4  `full vs FULL` = 1.0 EXACTLY and `full vs HUMAN` is just an arm's A2, so `full` appears on both
    sides of Q1 and only one side of Q2. Q2 is therefore the cleaner of the two, and Q1 needs its
    floor built explicitly. **DERIVATION.**

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: D3. An arm that predicts the human well will agree with
`full` well, so the released core's 0.7850 may be entirely explained by its A2. CONTROL IN THE SAME
ITERATION: regress `vs FULL` on `vs HUMAN` across all 20 distinct objects and report `coval_core`'s
RESIDUAL, with the residual's own null from the same regression. A residual at zero means Q1's raw
number is an A2 restatement and the clause is untested, not confirmed.

WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · THE CLAUSE IS FALSE ON ITS OWN TERMS. Q1 fails: `coval_core`'s agreement with `full` does not
    exceed its floor once D3's confound is controlled. Then "preserves ITS verdicts" is untrue and
    the retirement stands, by a route that needs no normalisation.
B · THE CLAUSE STANDS AND ITS REFUTATION WAS THE ARTIFACT. Q2 fails: the core does NOT beat the
    rubric at predicting the human. Then `whose_verdicts`'s WORLD B was produced entirely by the
    cross-target normalisation.
C · ⭐ THE FORK IS FALSE. Both Q1 and Q2 resolve positive. Then a core preserves the rubric's
    verdicts AND beats the rubric at predicting the human -- two compatible facts that the
    cross-target comparison forced into a dichotomy. The normalisation question dissolves rather
    than being answered, and that is an ontology change, not a parameter update.

  B -> Q2's paired difference CI contains 0 or is negative
  C -> Q1 resolves positive (raw AND residual) AND Q2 resolves positive
  any other pattern -> report; CLAIM NO WORLD.
