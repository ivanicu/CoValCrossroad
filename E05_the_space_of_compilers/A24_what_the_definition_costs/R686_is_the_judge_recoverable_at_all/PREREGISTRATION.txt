R686 · PRE-REGISTRATION

⛔ WHY R685's NEXT IS NOT RUN AS WRITTEN — IT IS A DERIVATION, NOT A TEST.
  It asked how many of the 81 UNRECORDED rounds store per-judge ranks or means. R684 DEFINED
  UNRECORDED as "no judge key appears in the artifact". A per-judge mean is stored under a judge key.
  So the answer is ZERO by construction, and the algebra forces it. "Could this have come out
  otherwise?" No. Reporting it as a finding would be 1+1=2 therefore 2<3 — the trap the standard
  opens with, and I wrote the NEXT line that walked into it.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY SEPARABLE, AND IS REGISTERED
  R684's key test was EXACT-NAME. A judge can be encoded other ways: in a FILENAME, in a `model` /
  `pool` / `variant` field whose VALUE names a judge, or embedded inside an arm or path string. None
  of those would have matched. So: of the 81, from how many is the judge RECOVERABLE by any
  encoding other than a top-level judge key?
POINT A     20 of the 81            INTERVAL A [5, 50]
POINT B     of those recovered, the most common encoding is the FILENAME.
DIRECTIONAL (separately failable) filename encoding is more common than value-field encoding.
KILL        if ZERO are recoverable, the judge is genuinely absent from those artifacts and "the
            corpus lost its scope" is the answer rather than "it did not print it".
CONTROLS
  ARITHMETIC (a derivation, labelled) confirm none of the 81 carries a top-level judge key. This
            CONFIRMS R684's partition; it is not evidence and no claim rests on it.
  POSITIVE  a synthetic artifact with the judge in its FILENAME must be recovered.
  g=0       a synthetic artifact with no judge anywhere must NOT be recovered — the recoverer must
            return both values.
  NEGATIVE  a value that merely CONTAINS "2B" inside a longer token (e.g. "d2Bx") must not match —
            a substring is not an encoding.
  PLACEBO   run twice identical.
IDENTIFICATION LIMIT  "recoverable" means a judge name is findable in the artifact's own bytes or
            filename. It does not establish that the recovered judge is the one that produced the
            verdict. Upper bound on recoverability, stated as one.
