  It proposes extending QUANT with ranking adjectives. But R707 measured that **0.758 of this gate's
  flagging survives scrambling the word order within each paragraph** — the detector is largely
  responding to two words landing within 60 characters BY CHANCE. Adding trigger words to a
  76%-chance detector plausibly adds mostly chance. ⭐ So the question is not "does the extension
  catch the missed case" (it will, by construction — `weakest` is the word) but "IS THE RANKING
  CLASS A BETTER TRIGGER CLASS THAN THE ONE ALREADY THERE?" That must be settled before shipping,
  and it is settled with R707's own instrument.

  Fully identified: deterministic functions of the corpus and of two word lists.
  ⚠ (ii) is a property of THIS corpus's prose; a chance share is not a property of the words.

WORLDS

CONTROLS
  POSITIVE   the ranking class must flag ledger 893's actual sentence, whose text is on record.
  g=0        with every ranking word deleted from the corpus, the ranking class must produce 0 new
             flags. A class that still fires is matching something other than its own words.
  NEGATIVE   within-paragraph word scramble — length and vocabulary preserved exactly, syntax and
             proximity destroyed. The world it excludes: "the new flags are driven by the ranking
             word standing in a real relation to an artifact noun".
  SHAM       a matched-size class of NON-RANKING adjectives {recent initial current standard typical
             ordinary partial further separate additional}. Same operation minus the ingredient
             (ranking-ness), size-matched at 10 words. If it flags comparably, ranking is not what
             is being measured.
  PLACEBO    two identical runs differ by exactly 0.
  NOISE FLOOR the shuffle rate's spread over >=900 permutations, measured not assumed.
  UNIT       instrument unit != claim unit, asserted and carried into the verdict.
