R797 · is the prompt's own rubric a WORSE human predictor than a generic pool — and does R796's
       inverse relation exist at all?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-06.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R796's NEXT proposed asking what distinguishes `coval_core` from `topvar_k4` -- "an arm whose
rubric-preference is +0.1608 higher and whose agreement with humans is 0.0802 lower."

CHECK #399 attacked it and found TWO problems.

(a) ⛔ THE PAIR IS NOT A LIVE QUESTION. `coval_core` and `topvar_k4` differ by 0.0802 on A2 against
    an MDE near 0.011, and R789 already placed them in DIFFERENT LEVELS (topvar_k4 in the 9-arm floor
    cluster at 0.4863, coval_core at 0.5665). They are among the best-separated pairs in the
    population. The stuck pair was `topw_k4`, not this one.
(b) ⛔ AND THE INVERSE RELATION IT GENERALISES FROM IS TWO ARMS. Measured across all 27 named arms,
    `corr(rubric-preference, A2) = -0.2550`. At n = 20 distinct objects that is nowhere near
    resolved. **R796's closing sentence built a pattern out of the two arms at the ends of it** --
    §4's *the closing sentence is a claim and never gets a control*, in my own last round.

⭐ BUT THE CHECK SURFACED SOMETHING SHARPER, AND IT IS A STATEMENT ABOUT THE RELEASE. The targets
themselves differ as human predictors: `genericpool16` scores **A2 0.5422** while `full` scores
**0.5087** (R793). If that gap is real, then **a generic 16-criterion pool predicts a prompt's own
human rankings better than the prompt's own rubric does** -- which explains R796 mechanically
(arms resembling a better predictor predict better) and is worth more than the correlation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  THE TARGET GAP IS A SAME-TARGET COMPARISON. `full vs HUMAN` and `genericpool16 vs HUMAN` are both
    proportions against the human annotators, so the comparison needs no ceiling and is paired per
    prompt. **DERIVATION** (R794's D1, reused), and it is why this estimand is clean where the
    ceiling questions were not.
D2  A CORRELATION AT n = 20 IS WEAK BY CONSTRUCTION. |r| must reach about 0.44 for a two-sided test
    at n = 20 to exclude zero. So -0.2550 is uninformative unless the design's MDE is reported
    beside it. **DERIVATION**, and a null without it is silence.
D3  THE ALIASES INFLATE n. R789 measured 27 named arms collapsing to 20 distinct objects; computing
    a correlation over 27 counts seven arms twice. **DERIVATION**, and the correlation must be
    recomputed on the 20.
D4  IF ARMS WERE MIXTURES OF `full`-LIKE AND `pool`-LIKE CLASSES, an inverse relation between
    rubric-preference and A2 would arise BY CONSTRUCTION whenever the pool is the better predictor.
    **DERIVATION**, and it is why E4's synthetic control decides whether the correlation is a
    finding or a restatement.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: `genericpool16` has 16 criteria and `full` has a mean of
15.48, but `full`'s count VARIES per prompt (min 4, max 39) while the pool's is fixed. A prompt whose
rubric has 4 criteria produces a noisier class than one with 39, so the gap could be a size-variance
effect rather than a quality one. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: stratify the gap by the prompt's own
criterion count and report the curve, plus the gap restricted to prompts with 12-20 criteria where
the two targets are closest in size.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMANDS (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  ⭐ THE TARGET GAP: `genericpool16 vs HUMAN` minus `full vs HUMAN`, paired per prompt over all
    annotators, with a cluster-bootstrap CI and its own MDE.
E2  ⭐ THE GAP STRATIFIED by the prompt's `full` criterion count -- the registered confound.
E3  R796's INVERSE RELATION, tested properly: `corr(rubric-preference, A2)` over the **20 distinct
    objects**, with a bootstrap CI over PROMPTS and an arm-label permutation null, **and its MDE**
    (what |r| this design could have detected).
E4  the derivation check: synthetic arms built as mixtures of `full`-like and pool-like classes, run
    through the identical pipeline. If they reproduce the observed correlation, E3 is a restatement
    of E1 and not an independent finding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · THE RUBRIC IS THE WORSE PREDICTOR. E1's gap is positive and resolved, and survives the
    size-stratification. Then the release ships a rubric that predicts its own prompt's human
    rankings worse than a generic pool does, and every "preserves the rubric" clause inherits it.
B · THE GAP IS A SIZE ARTEFACT. E1 resolves but vanishes on the 12-20 criterion stratum. Then it is
    about how many criteria a prompt happens to have, not about the rubric.
C · NO GAP. E1 is unresolved. Then R796's mechanism has no basis and the -0.2550 correlation is the
    only thing on the table -- which D2 says is uninformative at this n.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> E1 CI excludes 0, positive, AND the 12-20 stratum keeps the sign and resolution
  B -> E1 CI excludes 0 overall but the matched-size stratum is unresolved or reversed
  C -> E1 CI contains 0
  any other pattern -> report; CLAIM NO WORLD.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if OBJECT passes (`full vs HUMAN` reproduces R793's committed 0.5087 and `genericpool16`'s A2
   reproduces R789's committed 0.542233, both to 1e-9 -- exit 2 otherwise)
   and PLACEBO returns exactly 0 for a target against itself
   and POSITIVE fires with a computed band (a planted shift on one target's human column moves the
       gap monotonically and does not resolve at delta = 0)
   and NEGATIVE fires (shuffling the human targets across prompts collapses BOTH columns toward
       chance, so the gap is not a property of the targets alone):
        E1 unresolved                                       -> WORLD C   (checked FIRST)
        E1 resolved AND the 12-20 stratum keeps it          -> WORLD A
        E1 resolved AND the stratum loses it                -> WORLD B
        otherwise                                           -> report; claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OBJECT      two committed A2 values reproduced to 1e-9. Exit 2, never 0.
PLACEBO     a target against ITSELF as the human proxy: exactly 0 gap. A contrast with no effect.
POSITIVE    BAND COMPUTED AT BOTH ENDS: plant delta on `full`'s human column, sweep
            {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05}; require no resolution at 0 and resolution at the top.
NEGATIVE    shuffle the HUMAN classes across prompts. Both columns must collapse toward chance and
            the gap toward 0. World it excludes: "the gap is a property of the two targets' internal
            structure rather than of how they track THIS prompt's humans."
CONFOUND    E2's size stratification, registered above.
NOISE FLOOR MEASURED: annotator split-half on each target's human column.
E3 MDE      the |r| this design could detect at n = 20, by simulation, reported WITH the result.
UNIT        instrument unit = a (prompt, annotator) judgement; claim unit = a TARGET for E1 and an
            ARM for E3. Named separately, required unequal.
