R799 · is the rubric's sub-coin accuracy UNIFORM or CONCENTRATED — and can that even be asked?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-06.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R798 measured `coval_full`'s singleton criteria at accuracy **0.4805** against `genericpool16`'s
**0.5332**, gap +0.0538 resolved. Its NEXT proposed sorting the 14,984 instances by their own
accuracy and reporting what share are actively anti-predictive.

⛔ CHECK #401 KILLED THAT DESIGN BEFORE IT WAS BUILT, WITH ARITHMETIC AND NO DATA FITTING.
Each `full` criterion instance is ONE prompt's measurement: **5.73 non-tied pairs x 16.1 annotators
= 92 draws**, so one instance's accuracy has an SE near **0.0521** at p = 0.5. Therefore:

  **if EVERY criterion were truly at 0.4805, the observed share below 0.5 would be 0.646.**

R798's NEXT would have reported roughly two thirds of the rubric "anti-predictive" from **noise
alone**, with no inverted minority anywhere. That is §4's *conditioning on the outcome*: ranking
noisy estimates and reading a share off the ranking measures the noise as much as the object.
⚠ And the 0.0521 is OPTIMISTIC — annotators agree with each other, so the effective draws are fewer
than 92 and the true SE is larger, which makes the inflation worse rather than better.

⭐ THE IDENTIFIED VERSION IS A DECONVOLUTION. Split each instance's annotators in half and ask
whether half-1 accuracy predicts half-2 accuracy. If criteria genuinely differ in quality the two
halves correlate; if the spread is noise they do not. That needs no threshold and no ranking.

⭐⭐ AND THERE IS AN ASYMMETRY WORTH EXPLOITING. `genericpool16`'s 16 criteria each appear on ALL 968
prompts, so **each pool criterion IS well estimated** (n = 968) while no `full` criterion is (n = 1,
R798's D2). So "is the advantage concentrated?" is ANSWERABLE for the pool and NOT for the rubric,
and the round reports that asymmetry rather than pretending to one answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  OBSERVED VARIANCE = SIGNAL + NOISE. `var(observed) = var(true) + E[var(sampling)]`, so the true
    spread is `sqrt(max(var_obs - var_noise, 0))` and can be ZERO while the observed spread is large.
    **DERIVATION**, and it is why the naive share is inadmissible.
D2  SPLIT-HALF RELIABILITY ESTIMATES THE SAME THING WITHOUT A NOISE MODEL. `corr(half1, half2)` is
    positive only if `var(true) > 0`, and Spearman-Brown corrects it to the full-length reliability.
    **DERIVATION**, and it is the instrument that needs no assumption about the draw count.
D3  A POOL CRITERION IS ESTIMATED ON 968 PROMPTS AND A RUBRIC CRITERION ON 1. So the pool's
    per-criterion ranking is admissible and the rubric's is not, and NO amount of care makes the
    rubric's estimable from this release. **DERIVATION**, and it fixes what each half of the round
    may claim.
D4  A SPLIT-HALF ON ANNOTATORS MEASURES ONLY THE ANNOTATOR-SAMPLING COMPONENT. Variation across
    PROMPTS (a criterion's quality depending on which responses it faces) is inside "true" here, not
    inside "noise". **DERIVATION**, and it bounds the interpretation: reliability > 0 means "stable
    across annotators", not "a property of the criterion text".

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: the two halves of a prompt's annotators are not
independent -- annotators agree with each other, so a split-half correlation can be positive because
both halves see the SAME responses, not because the criterion has stable quality. CONTROL IN THE SAME
ITERATION: a synthetic population with ZERO true spread, built by resampling each instance's
judgements from a single common accuracy, run through the identical split-half pipeline. Its
reliability is what "no signal" looks like on this data's own correlation structure.

ESTIMANDS (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  ⭐ split-half reliability of per-instance accuracy, both pools, Spearman-Brown corrected, with a
    bootstrap CI and the zero-signal synthetic beside it.
E2  ⭐ the deconvolved spread: observed sd, estimated noise sd, and `sqrt(max(diff, 0))` -- with the

E3  ⭐ the pool's 16 criteria INDIVIDUALLY (n = 968 each, D3): accuracies, spread, and whether its

E4  the asymmetry itself, stated: what is estimable for the pool and not for the rubric, and why.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · CONCENTRATED. Split-half reliability is resolvedly above its zero-signal control for `full`, and
    the deconvolved spread is a material fraction of the observed one. Then criteria genuinely differ
    and "which ones" is a real question this release cannot answer (D3) but a future one could.
B · UNIFORM. Reliability is at or below the zero-signal control. Then the observed spread is noise,
    the rubric's criteria are interchangeable at this resolution, and R798's NEXT would have
    reported ~0.646 of a population that does not exist.
C · ASYMMETRIC. The pool shows resolved reliability and `full` does not. Then the answer differs by
    pool -- and since the pool is the one with 968 observations per criterion, C is partly a
    statement about POWER rather than about criteria, and must be said that way.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> `full` reliability CI excludes the synthetic control's, and deconvolved sd >= 0.3 x observed
  B -> `full` reliability CI contains or falls below the synthetic control's
  C -> pool reliability resolved above its control while `full`'s is not
  any other pattern -> report; CLAIM NO WORLD.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if OBJECT passes (both pools' mean accuracy reproduces R798's committed 0.4805 and 0.5332 to 1e-9 --
   exit 2 otherwise)
   and PLACEBO returns reliability 1.0 for a split of an instance against ITSELF
   and POSITIVE fires (a synthetic population with a KNOWN true spread returns a deconvolved
       estimate within 20% of it, at two different planted spreads -- band computed at both ends)
   and NEGATIVE fires (the zero-true-spread synthetic returns reliability indistinguishable from 0):
        pool resolved and `full` not                    -> WORLD C   (checked FIRST)
        `full` reliability above its control            -> WORLD A
        `full` reliability at or below its control      -> WORLD B
        otherwise                                       -> report; claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OBJECT      both mean accuracies against R798's committed values, to 1e-9. Exit 2, never 0.
PLACEBO     an instance split against itself: reliability exactly 1.0.
POSITIVE    BAND COMPUTED AT BOTH ENDS: synthetic populations with planted true sd in {0.02, 0.08};
            the deconvolution must recover each within 20%, and the two must differ.
NEGATIVE    the zero-true-spread synthetic, built from this data's own annotator structure. World it
            excludes: "a positive split-half correlation here can only mean the criteria differ."
NOISE FLOOR MEASURED: the analytic binomial SE (0.0521 from check #401) beside the empirically
            measured half-to-half sd, so the assumption in D1 is checked rather than asserted.
UNIT        instrument unit = a (prompt, criterion, annotator-half) accuracy; claim unit = a POOL;
            E3's unit = a POOL CRITERION. Three strings, required unequal.
