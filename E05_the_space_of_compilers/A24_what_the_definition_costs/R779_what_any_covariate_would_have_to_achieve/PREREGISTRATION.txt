R779 · the mediation bound — what ANY single covariate would have to achieve, and how far the best is
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-05.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R778's NEXT registered the realised Jaccard overlap between two arms' criterion sets against their
|d|, on the grounds that two `random_k` arms draw from the SAME rubric so their sets overlap and that
overlap is the variable each pair's difference actually sees.

CHECK #381 RAN IT, AND THE ANSWER IS A DECOMPOSITION RATHER THAN A HIT:
    within a family   corr(overlap, |d|)                 **-0.1270 to -0.1758**
    across families   corr(overlap_A, overlap_B)         **+0.8423 to +0.9304**
    conditioning M x R on the mean overlap                drop **1.0% to 1.9%**
So the four families share a near-common variable (overlap, r up to 0.93 -- far MORE shared than the
scales themselves at 0.52-0.60) which is nearly IRRELEVANT to the outcome (|d|, r = -0.15). A shared
driver transmits co-movement only in proportion to how much it DRIVES the outcome, and this one
barely does.

⭐ AND THAT IS AN ARITHMETIC LAW THIS THREAD HAS BEEN IGNORING FOR FOUR ROUNDS. For a covariate Z, the
   part of `corr(A, B)` that Z can account for is bounded by `corr(Z,A) * corr(Z,B)`. Checked against
   what is already committed:
     overlap        0.92 x (-0.15) x (-0.15) = 0.021   observed drop 1.0-1.9%   MATCHES
     rubricdisagree                0.33 x 0.36 = 0.119  observed drop 9.6%      MATCHES
   **The bound is tight in both cases, and it was computable before either round ran.**

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMAND (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  the mediation bound `corr(Z,A) * corr(Z,B)` against the MEASURED drop in `corr(A,B)` when
    conditioning on Z, for every covariate this thread has built: `poolspread` (R776),
    `orderdisagree` (R777), `n_rubric`, `rubricdisagree`, `rubricspread` (R778), and realised
    `overlap` (here). Six covariates x three M x R pairs.
E2  the REQUIRED correlation: for a single covariate to explain a co-movement of `r`, it needs
    `corr(Z, each scale) >= sqrt(r)`. With r = 0.59 that is **0.77**. Reported against the best
    achieved, so the shortfall is a number rather than an impression.
E3  whether ANY LINEAR COMBINATION of the six does better -- the multiple correlation of the six
    covariates with each family's scale, which bounds what the whole set can explain jointly and is
    the honest ceiling for "keep trying covariates".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDENTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1, E2 exact -- both sides are computable correlations.
E3 IDENTIFIED but OPTIMISTIC BY CONSTRUCTION: a multiple correlation fitted on the same 968 prompts it
   is evaluated on is an in-sample ceiling, so it OVERSTATES what the covariate set can do. That makes
   it a valid UPPER bound on the covariate programme and an invalid estimate of its performance, and
   the round reports it only as the former.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  THE MEDIATION BOUND. Partialling Z out of corr(A,B) removes `corr(Z,A)*corr(Z,B)` from the
    numerator, so a covariate weakly related to either scale cannot explain much of their
    correlation HOWEVER strongly the two families share it. **This is algebra**, and the round's
    contribution is to CHECK it against six measured drops, not to discover it.
D2  THE REQUIRED CORRELATION IS sqrt(r) IN THE SYMMETRIC CASE. To account for 0.59 a single covariate
    needs about 0.77 with each scale. Also algebra. The measurement is the SHORTFALL.
D3  OVERLAP IS SHARED MORE THAN THE SCALES ARE (0.92 vs 0.59) AND STILL EXPLAINS NOTHING. That is the
    counter-intuitive cell and it is the reason D1 is worth stating: sharing is not mediation, and
    four rounds of this thread searched for shared variables without checking whether they drive the
    outcome.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: a covariate could mediate NON-LINEARLY, in which case a
Pearson partial understates it and the bound does not apply. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: report
the SPEARMAN version of every correlation beside the Pearson one, so a monotone-but-curved relation
is visible; a genuinely non-monotone mediator would still escape, and that is in the register.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · THE BOUND HOLDS AND CLOSES THE PROGRAMME. Measured drops match `corr(Z,A)*corr(Z,B)` for all six
    covariates, the best single covariate is far below sqrt(0.59), and the in-sample multiple
    correlation is also below it. Then no covariate of this kind can explain the co-movement, the
    search is closed with a number, and the residual is a property of the arms rather than of any
    prompt-level variable available here.
B · A COVARIATE BEATS ITS BOUND. Some measured drop materially exceeds the product, which would mean
    the partial is picking up something the two-path decomposition misses -- suppression, or a
    non-linearity -- and the bound is not the right instrument.
C · THE JOINT SET REACHES THE THRESHOLD. The in-sample multiple correlation exceeds sqrt(0.59) for a
    majority of families, in which case the programme is not closed and the next step is an
    out-of-sample check of the combination rather than another single covariate.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> every |measured drop - product| < 0.05 AND best single < 0.40 AND multiple R < 0.77
  B -> some measured drop exceeds its product by >= 0.05
  C -> multiple R >= 0.77 for >= 4 of 6 families

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if POSITIVE fires (a SYNTHETIC covariate constructed to mediate a known share recovers that share
   within tolerance) and g=0 fires (a covariate independent of both scales removes ~nothing)
   and PLACEBO fires (a family against itself = 1.0):
        some drop exceeds its product by >= 0.05  -> WORLD B. The bound is the wrong instrument.
        multiple R >= 0.77 for >= 4 families      -> WORLD C. The programme is not closed.
        otherwise, if A's three conditions hold   -> WORLD A. The covariate search is closed with a
                                                    number, and the residual is named as arm-level.
        else                                      -> report; claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POSITIVE  build `Z = a*scale_A + b*scale_B + noise` with a KNOWN implied product, and require the
          measured drop to match it within 0.05. BAND, COMPUTED: at a = b = 0 the drop must be ~0
          (g=0 below); at large a, b it must approach the full correlation. The threshold sits
          strictly between two computed ends.
g=0       a covariate drawn independently of both scales: measured drop must sit inside the negative
          control's interval.
NEGATIVE  permute each covariate across prompts, 200 draws, and report the drop distribution -- the
          world excluded is "conditioning on ANY vector removes this much".
SHAM      ingredient ABSENT, not inverted. Ingredient = THE COVARIATE'S RELATION TO THE SCALES. A
          random vector with the same marginal distribution as the covariate, 200 draws.
PLACEBO   a family's scale against itself = exactly 1.0.
SPEARMAN  every correlation reported in both Pearson and Spearman form (the registered confound).
UNIT      instrument unit = a PROMPT; intermediate = an ARM PAIR; claim unit = a COVARIATE (6) and a
          FAMILY PAIR (3). All three named.
