R774 · is `c(p)` a property of the PROMPT or of the five arms that defined it?
WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE FOR THIS ROUND. 2026-08-05.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R772 named `c(p) = mean |d|` a per-prompt SEPARABILITY SCALE and R773 inherited that reading, closing
with "those prompts compress real arms much more than they compress a bad one" and registering the
sham-to-committed distance RATIO as a function of `c(p)` over the full 968.

⛔ CHECK #376: THE REGISTERED RATIO IS CONTAMINATED, AND THE ARITHMETIC SAYS SO.
   `c(p)` IS the committed-pair A2 distance, and the ratio's DENOMINATOR is the committed-pair
   satisfaction distance -- the same quantity in a different metric. Measured before designing:
   **corr(c, committed distance) = +0.2042**. So the regressor sits inside the denominator and the
   ratio trends downward by construction. **The clean form is two SEPARATE curves**, and running them
   changes the answer:

       c(p) quartile      n     c        committed    sham      ratio
       Q1               242   0.0003     0.0047      0.0354     7.47
       Q2               237   0.0200     0.0059      0.0376     6.32
       Q3               244   0.0486     0.0061      0.0313     5.15
       Q4               245   0.1124     0.0078      0.0340     4.34

   **corr(c, SHAM distance) = -0.0212 — the sham distance is FLAT across the whole range**, while the
   committed distance rises 1.7x. So the ratio's decline is entirely its denominator.
   ⇒ R773's reading -- "those prompts compress everything" -- is not what the full population shows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ESTIMAND (before method)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  the SHAM distance as a function of `c(p)`, as a curve over quartiles with each cell's interval --
    the test of whether prompts vary in how much they separate an ARBITRARY arm from the core.
E2  the COMMITTED distance as the same curve, reported beside it and never divided into it.
E3  ⭐ THE ONTOLOGY TEST. If `c(p)` is a property of the PROMPT, a scale computed from a DIFFERENT arm
    family must agree with it. If it is a property of THESE FIVE ARMS, it will not. Estimand: the
    correlation between `c(p)` from the committed five and `c'(p)` from a disjoint arm family
    (`random_k4_s0/s1/s2` plus `topabs_k4`, `topvar_k4`) which shares no member with the committed set.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDENTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1, E2 exact.
E3 IDENTIFIED provided the two arm families are DISJOINT -- otherwise a shared arm induces correlation
   for the same reason R771's arm-sharing pairs correlate. The families are checked disjoint in code
   and the round EXITS 2 if they are not.
⚠ AND THE CEILING ON E3 IS NOT 1.0. Even a perfect prompt property would correlate below 1 because
  each `c` is a noisy estimate; the round therefore reports the correlation ALONGSIDE the reliability
  bound obtained by splitting each family in half and correlating the halves -- the standard
  attenuation ceiling -- rather than against an imagined 1.0.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
population  968 prompts; committed family = the 5 extension members; comparison family = 5 arms
            sharing no member with it
instrument  A2 per prompt over all annotators; cosine distance on satisfaction vectors
baseline    none -- these are within-prompt comparisons
regime      first release, home judge, this tree_sha

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  A RATIO WHOSE DENOMINATOR TRACKS THE REGRESSOR TRENDS BY CONSTRUCTION. corr(c, committed) =
    +0.2042, so `sham/committed` must fall in `c` whether or not the sham varies at all. **Reporting
    that decline as "prompts compress everything" would be the same error as R773's NEXT.** The round
    reports the two curves separately and states the ratio only to show it is uninformative.
D2  THE TWO FAMILIES' SCALES SHARE A PROMPT-DIFFICULTY COMPONENT ONLY IF ONE EXISTS. Nothing forces a
    positive correlation between disjoint families' `c`, because each is a within-family DIFFERENCE
    magnitude and differencing removes what is common (R771's result, used here as a tool).
    ⇒ E3's correlation is a genuine measurement, not an artifact of shared difficulty.
D3  ATTENUATION. Two noisy estimates of the same latent quantity correlate at most at the geometric
    mean of their reliabilities, so E3 must be read against a measured split-half ceiling.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: the comparison family is made of weaker arms, so its `c'`
may be dominated by a different regime (all arms bad) rather than by prompt structure. CONTROL IN THE
SAME ITERATION: report each family's mean A2 level and its `c` distribution, so a regime difference is
visible rather than assumed absent.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A · `c` IS A PROMPT PROPERTY. The two disjoint families' scales correlate substantially relative to
    the split-half ceiling. Then "some prompts separate cores" is real and R772's name was right.
B · `c` IS AN ARM-SET PROPERTY. The correlation is near zero against a healthy ceiling. Then `c(p)`
    measures how close THESE FIVE happen to be on that prompt, R772's "separability scale" is a
    misnomer, and R773's tie finding is about the five arms rather than about the prompts.
C · UNIDENTIFIED. The split-half ceiling is itself near zero, so neither family's `c` is reliable
    enough to correlate with anything and the question cannot be asked at this n.

PREDICTION MATRIX
  A -> corr(c, c') >= 0.5 x the split-half ceiling
  B -> corr(c, c') <= 0.2 x the ceiling, with the ceiling >= 0.4
  C -> ceiling < 0.4

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KILL -- CONDITIONAL, gated on the controls
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
if DISJOINTNESS holds (exit 2 otherwise) and POSITIVE fires (a planted prompt-level scale makes the
   two families' `c` correlate) and g=0 fires (no planted scale -> no correlation) and PLACEBO fires
   (a family correlated with ITSELF returns 1.0):
        ceiling < 0.4                          -> WORLD C. The question is unanswerable at this n.
        corr >= 0.5 x ceiling                  -> WORLD A. `c` is a prompt property.
        corr <= 0.2 x ceiling                  -> WORLD B. `c` is an arm-set property; rename it.
        otherwise                              -> report the number, claim no world.
else:
        UNVERIFIED. Never OVERTURNED, never CONFIRMED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISJOINT   the two arm families must share no member. Asserted; the round EXITS 2 if violated,
           because a shared arm manufactures the correlation E3 is measuring.
POSITIVE   plant a prompt-level scale `s(p)` multiplying BOTH families' difference vectors, sweep the
           width in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0}, and require corr(c, c') to rise monotonely and be detected at
           1.0. BAND, COMPUTED: at width 0 the correlation must sit at its unplanted value; at 1.0 it
           must exceed the g=0 upper interval. Threshold strictly between two computed ends.
g=0        width 0 on simulated independent arms: corr(c, c') must sit at ~0 with its interval.
NEGATIVE   permute prompts in one family only, 200 draws, and report the correlation distribution.
           World excluded: "any two |d| vectors of this shape correlate".
SHAM       ingredient ABSENT, not inverted. Ingredient = THE SECOND FAMILY BEING A DIFFERENT ARM SET.
           Split the COMMITTED five into two overlapping halves and correlate their scales -- same
           machinery, but the arms are shared, so this measures what sharing alone buys.
PLACEBO    a family's scale correlated with ITSELF must be exactly 1.0.
NOISE      the split-half reliability of each family's `c` is measured, not assumed, and every
           correlation is reported against it (D3).
UNIT       instrument unit = a PROMPT; claim unit = the SCALE, whose ownership -- prompt or arm-set --
           is the round's question. Both named.
