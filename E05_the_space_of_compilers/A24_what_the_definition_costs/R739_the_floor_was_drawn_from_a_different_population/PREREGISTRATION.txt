⛔ MY PROPOSED STATISTIC WAS THE WRONG ONE, AND select_core.py SAYS SO IN ITS OWN COMMENT
   R738's NEXT proposed comparing the SATISFACTION LEVEL of a rule's selected criteria against the
   pool. The selection source carries a derivation that makes level the wrong quantity:
     "a criterion whose satisfaction is IDENTICAL across the four responses adds the same constant
      to every y_x, so it changes no pairwise sign and is arithmetically INERT no matter how
      important it is."
   The margin correlations this arc measures are built from across-response structure, so an inert
   criterion contributes nothing to them. The quantity that matters is the VARIANCE OF SATISFACTION
   ACROSS RESPONSES, not its level. A rule fitting the human target cannot afford inert criteria; a
   random subset takes them at the pool rate.

ESTIMAND
   (1) DERIVATION, labelled: zero across-response variance implies zero contribution to any pairwise
       comparison. Not evidence; it is why (2) is the right statistic.
   (2) MEASUREMENT: do rule-produced arms select criteria with systematically higher across-response
       variance than the pool, and by how much per rule?
   (3) CONSEQUENCE: under a floor built from random subsets MATCHED on that variance as well as on
       overlap, do R738's ten negative excesses change sign?

IDENTIFICATION
   (1) exact. (2) identified: every selected criterion's satisfaction across the four responses is on
       disk, and so is the pool's. (3) identified only where the pool contains enough criteria in the
       real arm's variance band to draw a matched subset; prompts that cannot supply one are reported
       as UNMATCHABLE and excluded, with their count, rather than back-filled from the whole pool.
   ⚠ NOT identified: whether variance is the ONLY property the rules select on. Matching on it
   removes one confound and cannot remove the ones nobody has named.

                across-response variance of satisfaction · baseline R738's overlap-matched floors ·
                regime default emitter
