R808 · DOES R807's SCALE SURVIVE ITS OWN PRECISION SWEEP — and does the leak proxy's IDENTITY matter?
written before the run · NBOOT 1200 · ZEFF 2.801585

PROVOCATION
R807 built a disattenuated scale (honest floor ~0.34, pure copy 1.000, fitted arms 0.50-0.65) by
dividing an observed slope by lambda = 0.4597. Its NEXT proposed sweeping the number of PARITY-1
annotators "as lambda improves". CHECK #410 read R807's own definition: lambda is the slope of the
leak's margin on parity-0 half B against half A -- it is the reliability of the EVALUATION draw, not
of the proxy. More parity-1 annotators change WHAT THE LEAK IS; more parity-0 annotators change HOW
NOISILY IT IS MEASURED. The NEXT conflated two axes, so this round separates them.

⛔ AND THE SWEEP IS THE §4 REMEDY, AIMED AT MY OWN HEADLINE. "a control validated by its own
instrument's noise": if an estimate moves as the instrument sharpens, the correction was never
correcting anything. R807's whole scale is one division by an estimated lambda and has never been
swept.

ESTIMAND
A-AXIS (evaluation precision). Hold the y-side (the arm's annotators) FIXED at one half of parity-0.
  Vary the x-side (the leak's annotators) over k = 1,2,3,4 drawn from the complementary half.
  Estimate lambda_k on the same k. Report the DISATTENUATED slope at each k.
  ⭐ DERIVED: noise in y does not bias a slope; only noise in x attenuates it. So holding y fixed
  and varying x isolates attenuation, and the disattenuated slope must be FLAT in k if the
  correction works. This is the estimand R807's headline rests on.
B-AXIS (proxy identity). Build the leak's modal class from j = 1,2,4,8 PARITY-1 annotators. This
  changes the object, not its measurement error. Report the disattenuated slope at each j.
  ⭐ DIFFERENTIAL PREDICTION: the FITTED arms were fitted on the FULL parity-1 set, so their slope
  should RISE with j and peak at j = all. The HONEST arms never saw parity-1, so theirs should be
  FLAT in j. That contrast is a leak test no previous round has run.

IDENTIFICATION
A-axis identified: lambda_k and the arm slope share no annotator by construction.
B-axis identified as a comparison BETWEEN arms; the level at any single j is not interpretable on
its own, because m1_j is a different object at each j.
⚠ Small k means a very noisy x, so lambda_k is itself estimated with error and appears in a
DENOMINATOR. Ratios to a small noisy number are unstable: the interval at k=1 is expected wide and
is reported, never smoothed.

DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
D1 [DERIVATION] lambda_k increases with k. If it does not, the split is not doing what I think.
D2 [DERIVATION] the raw (un-disattenuated) slope also increases with k, for the same reason. So the
   RAW slope drifting is NOT evidence against the scale; only the DISATTENUATED slope drifting is.
D3 [DERIVATION] the leak regressed on itself, same draw, is exactly 1.000 at every k. PLACEBO.
D4 the honest arms' B-axis profile is the floor for what "sensitivity to j" costs with no fitting.

WORLDS
A-STABLE   the disattenuated slope is flat in k -> R807's scale survives its own precision sweep.
A-DRIFT    it drifts monotonically in k -> the disattenuation is not correcting and R807's 0.50-0.65
           are artifacts of one lambda estimate.
B-SPECIFIC fitted arms rise with j, honest arms flat -> the fitted arms track the SPECIFIC labels
           they were fitted to, which is the sharpest leak evidence available here.
B-GENERIC  both flat in j -> the leak proxy's identity does not matter and "the leak" is really
           general annotator consensus.
CHECKED FIRST: A-DRIFT, because it destroys the previous round's headline.

fitted slope vs j           n/a           n/a           rises          flat
honest slope vs j           n/a           n/a           flat           flat

         A calibration that holds at one k and not another is the drift this round is looking for.
         g=0 check: the PURE leak copy must land at 1.000 at every k, and if it does not, the
         disattenuation is broken at that k and the cell is UNVERIFIED rather than reported.
