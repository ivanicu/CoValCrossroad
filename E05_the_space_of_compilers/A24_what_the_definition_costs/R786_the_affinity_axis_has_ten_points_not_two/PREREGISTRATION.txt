--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R785 measured that `coval_core` paraphrases its rubric (affinity 0.4951 against a 0.0555 null) while
`gen` does not (0.0865), and that the two arms' clause-② standing runs the same way (q_resolved 0.9978
against 0.0396). It then reported **"arms with criterion TEXT available: 2"**, declared a correlation
undefined at n = 2, and closed by saying the axis could not be extended on this site.

⛔ CHECK #388: **THAT POPULATION WAS A HARD-CODED DICT.** R785's E3 reads
`q782 = {"coval_core": 0.9978, "gen": 0.0396}` — a literal I typed. It never enumerated anything, so
its "n = 2" could not have come out otherwise. **A check that cannot fail, in the round that caught
its own placebo.**

⭐ ENUMERATED FROM THE OBJECT INSTEAD: of the arms carrying a `core_*.json`, **9 have criteria that
are NOT the rubric's** (mean verbatim overlap with `core_full.json` below 0.5): `gen`, `generic`,
`genericpool16`, `promptecho`, `coval_core_sham`, `full_sham`, `gen_sham`, `promptecho_sham`,
`topw_k4_sham`. Adding `coval_core`, whose texts live in the release rather than a core file (R782),
gives **10**. Every other arm with text is rubric-derived at verbatim ≥ 0.986 — affinity 1.0 by
construction, which R785 asserted correctly and which this round verifies rather than assumes.

⇒ **The correlation R785 called undefined is computable**, and the hypothesis it raised — that clause
② may score rubric affinity rather than quality — becomes testable rather than merely available.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARITHMETIC, DERIVED BEFORE MEASURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  A RUBRIC-DERIVED ARM HAS AFFINITY 1.0 BY CONSTRUCTION. Its criteria ARE rubric criteria, so the
    best match is the criterion itself. **DERIVATION**, and it is used as a POSITIVE CONTROL on the
    affinity instrument rather than reported as a result — an instrument that fails to return 1.0
    there is broken.
D2  A SHAM ARM SITS AT q_resolved 0.0000 BY CONSTRUCTION and, being built to break the ingredient,
    also sits low on affinity. **Including shams could therefore manufacture a correlation from two
    facts that are both definitional.** The specification is registered here: the correlation is
    computed BOTH ways and the sham-free figure is the one the claim rests on.
D3  A CORRELATION OVER SINGLE-DIGIT n HAS AN ENORMOUS MDE. With n arms the MDE on Pearson r at
    z_eff = 2.80 is roughly `tanh(2.80 / sqrt(n - 3))`, which for n = 8 is about 0.87 and for n = 5
    about 0.94. **So a "strong correlation" here is nearly uninformative and a null is silence.**
    Registered before the number so the report cannot be written as though r settled anything.
D4  R785's n = 2 WAS NOT A MEASUREMENT. A literal dict cannot return a different value, so its
    population claim had no severity. **DERIVATION about my own code**, and the reason this round
    counts in code and prints the count beside the list.

STRONGEST CONFOUND, written BEFORE the run: affinity and q_resolved may both be driven by ARM QUALITY
— a good arm scores well on the judge and also happens to be written in rubric-like language — so a
correlation would be quality, not affinity. CONTROL IN THE SAME ITERATION: report each arm's raw A2
beside both quantities, and give the affinity-to-q_resolved relation with A2 held at its observed
spread, so a reader can see whether the ordering is anything but the A2 ordering.

WORLDS

  B -> sham-free relation reverses or vanishes
  C -> the sham-free intersection has n < 5, where D3's MDE exceeds 0.94
