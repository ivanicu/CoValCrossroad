WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS -- it corrects the round I committed one step ago
   R729 reported that 7 arms today's population admits are built by a target-reading rule and that
   clause ③ excludes none of them. That count is over TAGS. R523 established, on a 56-tag universe,
   that oracle_k4_oracle_kA and oracle_k4_oracle_kB are EXACT ALIASES of oracle_k4 -- which IS on
   the blocklist -- and that all three A/B pairs are internally identical. R524 partitioned 56 tags
   into 46 objects. So the clause DOES exclude the oracle object; it merely fails to recognise two
   of its tags, which is a different and smaller defect than the one R729 asserted.
   ⛔ THE UNIT ERROR IS MINE AND IT IS THE THIRD IN THREE ROUNDS: R729 measured tags, R729's own
   B point measured a 9-way rule when the claim was binary, and R728's anchor measured a denominator
   the design could not return. Same shape each time.

ESTIMAND
   At the level of DISTINCT OBJECTS -- exact per-prompt satisfaction-vector identity, R523's own
   criterion -- how many target-reading objects does clause ③ admit in today's 92-tag population?
   And how many of R729's 7 tags survive as distinct un-excluded objects?

IDENTIFICATION
   Fully identified: two tags name the same object iff their satisfaction vectors are equal on the
   prompts they share, and that is computable from the sat store.
   NOT identified: whether two objects that are merely very close should count as one. This round
   uses EXACT equality, as R523 did, and reports the near-miss distribution so the choice is visible
   rather than hidden. A tolerance would be a different estimand and is not substituted silently.

SCOPE
   population : today's 92 tags and the 16 admitted, from corebench/results/sat_*.npz
   instrument : exact per-prompt satisfaction vector equality over shared prompts
   baseline   : R523's finding on 56 tags (3 identical pairs, 2 aliases of a declared arm)
   regime     : this tree_sha
