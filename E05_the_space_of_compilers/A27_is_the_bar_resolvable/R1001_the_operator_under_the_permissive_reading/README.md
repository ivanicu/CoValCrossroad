# R1001 · under the permissive reading the operator admits NOTHING — because clauses ③ and ④ are disjoint

**THE DECISION THIS MAKES SAFE.** Whether R1000's "the core is admitted by its own definition"
survives the other admissible reading of clause ④. **It does not**, and the reason is a conflict
between two clauses rather than a property of either.

---

## ⛔ First, the prior art — and how it was caught

**Entry 1368 and R824 already committed "the extension is EMPTY" under the permissive reading**, and
already said **"clause ④ as written cannot both do work and admit its own instance."**

⭐ **I found this because the currency gate refused to go red.** Registering the new fact should make
the gate FAIL until the statement is annotated; it passed on arrival, because the statement already
contained the sentence. **An instrument built to catch decoration caught a rediscovery instead** —
and the round's headline had to be demoted from a finding to a **confirmation by a different route**
(the full four-clause conjunction over 96 arms, swept over R826's plateau, rather than ④'s exclusions
in isolation).

## The confirmation

| | enumerated reading (R849) | **permissive reading (R825/R826)** |
|---|---|---|
| conjunction admits | 9 / 12 of 96 | **0 of 96**, in **20 of 20** saturated cells |
| `coval_core` in it | yes | **no**, in 20 of 20 |
| ④'s unique removals | **0** — inert | **9 / 12** — load-bearing |

**So R1000's inertness of clause ④ was a property of the READING, not of the clause.**

## ⭐ What is actually new — the mechanism

At R825's own k = 100 (bar **0.572551**), clause ④ admits **14** of the 96 arms:

```
④ admits             14
of which supervised   14     ⇒ ④ ∧ ③ = 0, before clause ② is even applied
```

⭐⭐⭐ **All 14 clear the bar by reading human rankings — exactly what clause ③ forbids.** The two
clauses have **disjoint satisfaction sets** on this release. Entry 1368 said ④ *cannot both do work
and admit its own instance*; this says **why**, and the reason is **not internal to ④**. It is a
direct conflict with a different clause. **The definition is not merely reading-dependent: under one
reading, two of its clauses cannot be jointly satisfied by anything at all.**

## Why this cost no compute

R1000's NEXT asked for *"a 99-arm scoring run at the permissive bar"*, and I was about to spend it.
**The attack ladder says arithmetic before compute**: R825's bar is already in mean-A2 units — it is
what R825 compared directly against `coval_core`'s 0.566477 — and R826 committed the whole curve. The
permissive extension is a **threshold on a quantity already in hand**. The expensive round was
unnecessary, and the specification curve came free.

## Controls

| control | result |
|---|---|
| **POSITIVE** (wiring) | R922's cut and count reproduced at 1e-9, both comparators |
| **POSITIVE** (k=0) | the no-modelling bar must **admit** the core — R826's own verdict there is *"admits core"* |
| **POSITIVE** ①③ | `topw_k1` fails ①; `oracle_k4` fails ③ |
| **NEGATIVE** | bar 0.0 admits all 96; bar 1.0 admits none. A threshold that does not sweep to both ends is not a threshold |
| **PLACEBO** | at k = 0, ④ has **0** unique removals. Non-zero there would mean the join was broken |

**Verdicts are taken only on R826's saturated cells (k ≥ 40).** The four unsaturated cells are printed
for contrast and used for nothing — below saturation a threshold measures modelling effort, not a
clause.

## ⚠ Impossible here, with what it would require

**A resolvable permissive ④ that carries the BAR's own error.** R826 reports the bar's sd
(0.0062–0.0079) but not R825's 12 per-split values, so the `lo > bar` shape carries the **arm's**
error only. **Direction named: conservative for admission, anti-conservative for exclusion** — an arm
called excluded here might survive once the bar's error is propagated. It would require R825's 12
per-split bar values, or a re-run that persists them.

⚠ Note the shape of that limitation: it works **against** this round's own conclusion, which is why
it is stated rather than filed.

## Alternatives considered
