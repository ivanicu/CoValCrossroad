⭐ **The `unanchored` column is all `yes` — 11 of 11.** A bare `\bR\d+\b` scan would have reported
**perfect self-checking**, because this project's commit bodies cite prior rounds constantly. The
anchored instrument — the id inside a sentence carrying a revisit verb — returns **8**. **The
difference between 11 and 8 is the entire methodological content of this round**, and it is the
lesson R998 learned one round earlier, applied to the round that learned it.

## ⚠ The three that stood, named

**R979** (19 chances), **R991** (7), **R994** (4). Note the top of the table: **the wall with the
most opportunity to be checked is the one that was not.** That is the opposite of what an
opportunity-driven story predicts, and it is why the share is reported beside the column rather than
as a flat rate.

## ⛔ My negative control was contradictory by construction, and its own run caught it

v1 made **R997 serve as both** the positive control (*must read revisited* — R998 corrects it by
name) **and** the "last round, no successor" negative (*must not*). **The same round cannot be both**,
and the run printed the contradiction as a control failure.

Worse, the intended control is **structurally unavailable**: every wall-declaring round here has at
least one later commit, so there is **no zero-opportunity case to test on**. It is named as
unavailable rather than quietly dropped — an unavailability claim in the flattering direction is
still an unavailability claim.

⭐ **Replaced with one that is available**: a round id appearing in no commit must read unrevisited.
That tests whether the classifier **invents** revisits, which is the failure mode that matters.

## ⚠ What this does not say

**It counts whether a wall was returned to, never whether the return was right.** R998 happens to
have overturned one; that is not evidence the other seven were handled well. A round that revisits a
wall and reaffirms it wrongly counts identically here.

## Alternatives considered

**Report 11 of 11 from the unanchored scan.** Refused — it is the exact instrument R998 rejected one
round earlier, and using it to flatter the loop's self-checking would be the cleanest possible
demonstration that the loop does not self-check.

**Drop the opportunity column and report 73%.** Refused: R997 had one chance and R979 had nineteen.
A flat rate over unequal exposure is a number with no scope, which is eleven of twelve retractions in
this project's own history.
